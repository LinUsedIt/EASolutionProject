## Summary

The OSM is a two-person office in CSA that implements UA&P's Student Mentoring Program, a university hallmark for the integral (holistic) formation of students. It does not run the mentoring itself. The schools pair students with mentors, mentors mentor, and the OSM keeps the program uniform across schools, approves and trains mentors, tracks pairings and sessions, and reports to the CSA Executive Director and ManCom. From AY 2026-27, mentoring became mandatory for undergraduates (3 sessions per semester) under a new university policy, and the OSM is in its first semester of enforcing it. Its two named pain points are **pairing** and **monitoring**. Almost everything it does runs on Google Sheets, Google Forms, an email script, and email chains. Everything it is doing this year is new, and nobody yet knows whether it will work (I1 [22:40], [50:53]).

## 1. Why the OSM exists (the WHY)

- **The program is a hallmark of UA&P.** It supplements quality education and integral formation. Its stated goals: (1) inform, orient, and guide students through university life and help them appreciate the university's educational principles and culture; (2) contribute to desirable personal habits and professional competencies; (3) contribute to integral formation by orienting students on academic growth, professional direction, and the move from late adolescence to young adulthood. I2 [00:04] [One source]
- **It is unusual.** UA&P is one of few universities with mentoring; most have only guidance and counseling. CHED's memorandum on student affairs does not include mentoring, so it is a value-added feature. I2 [11:26]; N2 [One source]
- **Holistic formation (CSA's definition):** the student's personal, professional, and social life; strengthening intellectual skills and moral criteria. CSA statement quoted by Ms. Nina: "The CSA is the guardian of the student value chain. UA&P's hallmark of values formation is promoted by CSA through the holistic formation of the student by the cultivation of university life outside the classroom as an effective complement to the academic course of study." I2 [13:11]-[19:01]
  - The unit could not give an indicator for "holistically developed". The answer was "very hard", with the aim that students "be upstanding individuals when they leave the university", and the suggestion that the team define it themselves. I2 [20:43], [22:16]. This is the gap Sir keeps probing (I2 [22:30]; S).
  - Dr. Olmos's view: holistic means total personal development: mental, spiritual, human, doctrinal, social, professional, and academic. I3 [10:24]. Dr. Lactao's view: the aspects are not silos; each influences the others. I4 p9. [One source each]
- **The OSM's role is implementation.** "We are required to implement it. We are just the implementors." I2 [25:50]. "We are policy implementers." I1 [67:05] [Confirmed]
- **Why a central unit exists.** If the unit did not exist, the schools would either stop offering the program or each run their own approach. The unit exists so implementation is "as much as possible uniform across all schools". Schools can pair students, but mentor training and development and institutional services are up to the OSM. I2 [26:08]-[26:57]; N2 [Confirmed]
- **Why mentoring became mandatory.** Before the policy it was voluntary. The university's new President is "very invested" and wants all students to engage and see the value. I2 [28:00]-[28:42]. Seeing the value means experiencing the program, not that the program is on probation. I2 [29:19]. [One source]
- **Why participation data matters.** If you cannot show data that mentoring is happening, "how can we claim that it's one of the best features of the university?" It is "more of evidence". I2 [39:32]-[39:57]. Dr. Olmos: if a student graduates without a single mentoring session, the university cannot say it gave that student a holistic formation. I3 [18:25]-[24:22]. [Confirmed in substance]
- **What 100% participation would bring:** backs the hallmark claim; makes it possible to host an international mentoring conference again; sets UA&P apart. I2 [40:48]
- **Where the unit wants to be in 1 to 3 years:** still within CSA under holistic formation; a strong program students enjoy, "not just because it's required"; an automated, "foolproof" way to match students and mentors (self-described as "a very lofty goal"). I2 [31:56]-[32:06], [58:08]-[58:38]. After the policy is running, the next step is to improve the quality of the mentoring relationship. I2 [23:15]
- **Who sets strategic direction:** a discussion between the unit, the CSA Operations Committee, and Management, in collaboration with schools. Schools differ in emphasis (professional growth, academics, mental-health prevention). I2 [33:05]. The Executive Director, not the operations staff, converses with ManCom. I2 [34:30]

## 3. The catalyst: the 3-session policy

- From AY 2026-27 undergraduates must have **at least 3 mentoring sessions per semester**. It is the first year this is required; it is the first time students (not mentors) log sessions. I1 [23:44], [50:53]-[51:06]; I2 [28:00]
- **The policy was released on August 4** (I2 [62:39]). The draft proposal says August 7. [Conflict]
- **Consequence of not meeting it:** the student is put on "follow-up" status; the OSM contacts them near the end of the semester and schedules sessions for them; failing for **two consecutive semesters** may affect eligibility to enroll in the next semester. I1 [24:25]; I2 [09:16]; N1 [Confirmed]. The Memory Bank and the draft proposal say **three** consecutive semesters. [Conflict]
- **It applies to students, not mentors.** I1 [23:44]
- **It is non-negotiable.** "The three sessions is non-negotiable." The unit expects to see "a lot of things" by the end of this semester, and is checking whether it is effective. I2 [64:31]; N2. Ms. Nina: "Right now, what I'm holding on to is the policy." I2 [61:53]
- **Timing guidance:** the first session should ideally happen within the first month (Sept); if not, the student can complete the three within the semester, given suspensions and scheduling. I1 [58:24]. Dr. Olmos described soft windows: opening to Sept 23 (just before midterms), after midterms to Nov 7, then the end of the semester. I3 [18:25]-[24:22]. [One source for the windows]
- **Reception by students:** older students may be less aware; new students reach out more. I4 p5. Some students skimmed the policy (Em said so himself in I1 [25:33]). Dr. Olmos sees freshmen engaging more, thanks partly to FreshStart orientation. I3 [43:58]. [One source each]

## 4. How the OSM works today (as-is processes)

### 4.1 Mentor approval and sourcing

- There is an **approved list of mentors**; students are assigned only to approved mentors. I1 [02:16]
- Eligibility: familiarity with the program and the university; academic and professional experience; plus an **internal scorecard that is strictly confidential** (exact criteria not shared). I2 [06:54]-[07:35]; N2. Dr. Lactao: part-time faculty can be mentors if recommended; someone who knows an external mentor must vouch for them; alumni are easier. I4 p7
- Faculty are normally recommended by their supervisor. Dr. Lactao's chain: supervisor to OpCom to OSM to ManCom, which approves the list. I4 p7 [One source; see section 13]
- External mentors are sourced through referrals: alumni, former professors, speakers, former employees. HR handles contracts, FMR handles allowances. I2 [07:35]; I1 [10:33]
- In principle all full-time faculty should do some mentoring. New hires are reviewed for the possibility of becoming mentors. I3 [08:49], [49:28]. [One source]
- Mentors who are "not a good fit" (based on information from students, ManCom, OpComs, faculty and staff) are not re-engaged the next school year. I2 [54:52]-[57:15]. [One source]
- **Known problems:** the proposed list was submitted early last cycle but approval was significantly delayed, which made pairing difficult. I1 [48:10]. Part-time faculty mentors may not have committed to continue. I1 [47:06]

### 4.2 Pairing (mentee to mentor)

- **The schools pair.** The OSM gives each school the approved mentor list. Coordinators usually pair with faculty or staff from their own school, so there is familiarity. Schools "know the students better". I1 [15:05], [18:46]; N1; I4 p1
- Schools can pair only after receiving the enrolled-student list from the Registrar. I1 [40:49]. This list is often late because enrollment gets extended. I2 [60:25]
- **Per-school practice differs:**
  - Each school appoints a male and a female coordinator in some schools; one coordinator for both in others. I1 [41:33]; I3 [02:12]
  - SSE (Dr. Olmos): start from the old list, remove graduates, keep the previous mentor unless the student or mentor wants a change, assign only incoming freshmen. Pairing basis is partly personal (e.g. IE boys with him; IT students with IT faculty) and limited by faculty capacity. I3 [24:31], [27:39]
  - School of Education (Dr. Lactao): emails students from last year's list asking whether to keep their mentor, plus a Google Form survey (only 10 to 20% answered); asks mentors whether they will take more mentees; the only matching criterion is the mentee's preference; unchosen mentees go to mentors willing to take more. After pairing, emails each mentor their mentee list; the OSM emails the students. I4 p1-p2, p4
  - Other schools: School of Management has far fewer mentors than students, so it asks mentors in other schools. I3 [27:39]
  - Matching criteria in general: mentor's subject or skill fits the student's course and/or familiarity. N1
- **The OSM's own role in pairing:** it keeps the pairing database; it evaluates change requests; it redistributes mentees when a mentor leaves (e.g. 8 mentees of a resigned faculty member: Dr. Olmos took 4, the other 4 went to others). It may also pair JC freshmen itself and then hand the database to schools. I1 [49:10] (Em was unsure if that was a proposal or an actual practice, N1). I3 [04:55]
- **Timing:** pairing takes on average a week or two, and for some schools as little as three days. It depends on school, coordinator availability, and volume. The target was "by next week" (as of 2026-09-11). I1 [41:33]-[42:22], [58:05]. Pairing should be finished by the start of the semester. Ideal plan: retain or pair upperclassmen by the end of the previous school year. I1 [46:10]; N1
- Pairing is hard because of requests ("this individual with this mentor only") and mentor preferences. Both staff called it "bloody/tedious". Ms. Nina: "There are many moving parts." I1 [42:39]; I2 [54:52]
- **Delivery:** the OSM informs students and mentors of pairings. This semester it also sends each mentor a summary of their mentees across all schools (a mentor may have mentees in several schools). I1 [26:41]-[27:07]
- **Mentors may ask for a short profile of the mentee before accepting.** I1 [53:54]

### 4.3 Change of mentor

- **Reasons:** no rapport or connection; the student found someone who may guide them better or heard a mentor is good; the mentor resigned or is on leave; no relationship was established. I1 [16:40]-[17:48]; N1. The surge came with the policy: students who never met their mentor now want a change, and some who did not know their mentor were "caught off guard" by the pairing. I2 [47:15]-[47:55]
- **Valid reasons** that warrant an immediate change: mentor resigned or on sabbatical. Requests that do **not** warrant it: the student hasn't met the mentor, or just wants a mentor they heard is good. I1 [44:14]-[44:51]
- **Steps (OSM view):** student contacts OSM (in person or email) or the coordinator; OSM evaluates; OSM talks to the current mentor, the proposed mentor (capacity), and the school; the school's reasons for the original pairing are respected; then the OSM updates its database, the school's database, and informs coordinators and both mentors. Too many emails. I1 [43:03], [44:03]-[44:51]; N1
- **Evaluation criteria** depend on the school (some want mentors from their own school), and on the availability and capacity of the requested mentor. No formal checklist found. I2 [48:43]
- **Variation:**
  - SSE (Dr. Olmos): students go to OSM or the coordinator; the coordinator tells OSM which students can be assigned to whom; OSM informs the student and mentors; takes about two days. I3 [31:03]-[34:08]
  - School of Education (Dr. Lactao): asks the student for a preferred mentor, asks that mentor, otherwise offers a list; then sends the updated list copying Ms. Nina or the OSM and emails the student. I4 p6
- **Final approval** is disputed. Ms. Nina corrected Em: it is not ManCom, "OSM lang". I2 [46:23]-[46:25]. N1 says the school has the final say. [Conflict]
- **Scale of problem:** "the biggest problem for us" right now. Ms. Nina received three change requests during the 2026-09-28 interview. She wants to automate it so the unit only gives final approval. I2 [43:31], [46:38], [54:52]
- **No strategy** to prevent change requests beyond matching as best as they can. I2 [57:59]; N2

### 4.4 Session logging and monitoring

- **Before:** a Mentoring Portal ran for many years; the mentor logged sessions. Not all mentors logged. It was built by Sir Pira (this team's professor). I1 [20:56]-[22:15]; I2 [36:55]; N1 ("double-edged sword"). The portal malfunctioned about two years ago; it can still be viewed but is less useful. Dr. Olmos kept using it and says it let him see other mentors' entries; errors occurred when graduated students were not removed. I1 [21:27]; I3 [46:06]-[48:38]. Last year the OSM introduced a Google Form for mentors. I1 [21:06]
- **Now (AY 2026-27):**
  1. The **student** logs the session with a Google Form (date, duration, where in the university, purpose, short survey on whether the session met the student's goal; no personal or confidential content). I1 [04:53], [19:25]
  2. A Google Apps Script emails the **mentor** to confirm details or request a correction; the mentor clicks Confirm and answers a required question on how the session went. A session counts only after mentor confirmation. I1 [34:27]-[37:37]; I3 [39:57]
  3. The confirmation is written to the form's response Google Sheet, and the student is notified. I1 [32:37]; N1
  4. A second email goes to a **dedicated admin email** as a monitoring backup, because the response sheet is shared among several people and accidental edits could affect enrollment eligibility. I1 [37:34]-[39:35]
  5. As of 2026-09-28 the IT Office has linked the form sheet to the pairing database so a confirmation automatically updates the student's session count. I2 [03:42]-[03:58]. On 2026-09-11 this was only an idea (I1 [32:37]-[34:10]).
- **Why the change from mentor to student:** mentors forget (Ms. Kelai has 90 mentees); students have one mentor; the policy applies to students; logging gives students proof; the unit wants students more involved. I1 [22:56]-[23:44]; I2 [36:55]
- **Past undocumented mentoring:** informal mentoring happened and was not logged, so required logging gives them the data. I1 [28:34]
- **Uncertainties:** first time students log; "we still don't know" if it is better. I1 [22:40]. Whether students will log properly is a named risk. I2 [60:25]. Group mentoring happens ("some mentors"), but there is no data. I2 [38:45]-[39:13]
- **What the OSM reports on:** frequency of sessions and the quality of mentoring (evaluation from students and mentors), as it expects ManCom to ask. I2 [34:53]; N2. Red flag that triggers leadership intervention: low mentoring logs (before and after the policy). I2 [36:44]
- **Coordinators** do not currently have access to the OSM databases. They had wanted to see which students in their school have logged; IT found it difficult, so the OSM will give summaries. I1 [65:49]-[66:46]
