# Sources and citations

## TOGAF sources

When you explain TOGAF, assess work, or draft anything, draw TOGAF content
**only** from these files in `Course Files/Readings/`:

1. **The TOGAF Standard, 10th Edition** (`The TOGAF Standard, 10th Edition.pdf`)
2. **The TOGAF Enterprise Architecture Foundation Study Guide** (Van Haren)
3. **Mastering the TOGAF Standard** by Eric Jager (2025): the full `.epub`,
   plus one PDF per chapter in `Mastering the TOGAF Standard - Segmented/`

The guides in `Course Files/Study Guides/` are summaries of these books.
Use them to find things quickly, but cite the underlying book and page.

## How to cite

Cite every TOGAF claim. Use short inline citations:

- `(Jager 2025, Ch. 8, p. 199)`
- `(TOGAF Standard 10th Ed., <part or section>)`
- `(Foundation Study Guide, Ch. 3)`

If something is **not** in the three Readings, say so plainly. If you then
add general knowledge, label it as such ("Not in our Readings; general EA
practice is..."). Never invent page numbers or section numbers.

Jager mixes what the Standard says with his own opinions. When it matters,
keep them apart ("Jager's view, not the Standard's").

## Things that are not TOGAF sources

- **Professor's instructions, assignment guides, rubrics, and feedback**
  define what is required. Use them for that, but never cite them as
  backing for a TOGAF concept, definition, or best practice.
- **Interview transcripts, recordings, and the team's notes** are evidence
  about the enterprise (CSA Mentoring Unit). Cite them by file name and
  date, for example `(2026-09-28 interview transcript)`.

## Large files

The Readings are large. Don't load whole books into the main conversation.
Use the `togaf-researcher` subagent to search them and bring back cited
excerpts.
